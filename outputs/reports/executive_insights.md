Top 10 Headline Findings

Finding 1 — Python Leads the Skills Race in Indian Tech Hiring
Python tops India's 2025-26 tech skill charts with 15.3% penetration, showing up in 4,959 of the 32,438 tech postings studied. Paired with SQL (9.6% penetration), these two skills together form the baseline requirement for most data-oriented roles, regardless of seniority.
Business Implication: L&D teams should treat Python + SQL as the go-to starter pair when onboarding tech freshers.

Finding 2 — Fresher-Eligible Roles Make Up Just One-Fifth of the Market
Defining "fresher" as 0-2 years of experience, only 6,556 of the 32,438 postings analyzed (20.21%) are actually open to new graduates. Most listings cluster around a 3-5 year requirement, effectively shutting the door on entry-level candidates.
Business Implication: Employers who market themselves as fresher-friendly should re-examine their JD experience floors — the numbers suggest most roles aren't really open to new grads.

Finding 3 — Bengaluru and Hyderabad Pay the Same, Statistically Speaking
Hyderabad edges out Bengaluru on median pay (₹13.5 LPA vs ₹13.2 LPA), but a Mann-Whitney U test (p = 0.474) shows the gap isn't statistically meaningful. Both cities comfortably out-earn Pune (p < 0.001).
Business Implication: New graduates shouldn't pick Bengaluru over Hyderabad (or vice versa) for pay reasons — compensation between the two is effectively identical.

Finding 4 — Seniority Pays Off 4.2x
Technical Leads and Architects earn a median ₹25 LPA — roughly 4.2 times what Software Engineers make (₹5.9 LPA). Salary climbs 373% across the first five years of a career (₹2.75 LPA → ₹13 LPA), making this the fastest-growing stretch of the whole trajectory.
Business Implication: Skill-building early in a career yields the biggest salary payoff — the first five years matter most financially.

Finding 5 — Accenture Leads Fresher Hiring by Volume
Accenture posts 1,088 fresher-eligible roles (19% of its total listings), making it India's biggest single source of entry-level tech openings by raw count. By contrast, Wipro (4.9%) and Infosys (6.1%) devote a much smaller share of postings to freshers than is commonly assumed.
Business Implication: New graduates chasing volume should look first to Accenture, IBM, and Google India rather than defaulting to TCS, Wipro, or Infosys.

Finding 6 — Generative AI Carries the Biggest Pay Premium
Among skills appearing across 3 or more role categories, Generative AI carries the largest salary premium — +150%, or ₹22.5 LPA against a ₹9 LPA baseline. AWS, Kafka, and Spark follow closely, each adding roughly +122%.
Business Implication: For salary growth, the smart upskilling sequence looks like: Python → SQL → AWS → Spark/Kafka → Generative AI, with each step adding measurable pay uplift.

Finding 7 — More Skills Listed Doesn't Mean More Pay
A Pearson correlation of r = 0.020 (p = 0.109) shows essentially no link between the number of skills tagged on a posting and its salary — partly a byproduct of Naukri's 8-skill cap limiting variance. That said, there is a threshold effect: postings with just 1-2 skills pay around ₹3.9 LPA, versus ₹9-11 LPA once a posting lists 3 or more.
Business Implication: A long skills list in a JD isn't a signal of higher pay. Freshers are better off prioritizing relevant skills over sheer quantity.

Finding 8 — Java, Spring Boot, and Microservices Form India's Tightest Skill Cluster
Jaccard similarity scoring identifies Spring Boot + Microservices (0.41) as the strongest skill pairing in the Indian market, with Java + Spring Boot (0.31) rounding out a tight backend trio that co-occurs in 31-41% of postings mentioning any one of the three.
Business Implication: Java backend developers should treat Spring Boot and Microservices as core, expected skills rather than nice-to-haves.

Finding 9 — Weekend Job Postings Fall Off a Cliff
Posting volume drops 97% on weekends — just 157 posts compared to a weekday peak of 5,712. Interestingly, the share of fresher-eligible roles is actually higher on weekends (30%) than on weekdays (18%), even though total volume is much lower.
Business Implication: Job seekers should focus applications Tuesday through Thursday for the widest selection, but shouldn't ignore weekend listings — fresher roles are proportionally more common then.

Finding 10 — No City Cracks the Top Tier for Fresher Opportunity
The Fresher Opportunity Index (FOI) — a weighted composite of entry-level ratio (40%), salary (30%), market depth (20%), and flexibility (10%) — shows that not a single Indian city reaches a score of 70 or above. Bengaluru and Hyderabad tie for the lead at 56.5, placing them in the "Strong" tier rather than "Top Tier."
Business Implication: India's tech job market structurally favors experienced hires. Even in the strongest cities, only 14-17% of roles are genuinely fresher-accessible, so portfolio work and demonstrable skills become critical differentiators for new graduates.

Methodology Notes

Data Source: Naukri.com India Job Market Dataset 2025-26
Tech Role Classification: Keyword-based classifier (v3), ~90% precision; 33.4% of raw data classified as tech roles
Fresher Definition: minimum experience ≤ 2 years
Salary Analysis: Based on 19.66% of postings with disclosed salary (6,376 records)
Statistical Tests: Mann-Whitney U for city comparison, Pearson correlation for skill-salary, Spearman for experience-salary
FOI Formula: Weighted composite of 4 normalized components — see kpi_definitions.md for full formula
